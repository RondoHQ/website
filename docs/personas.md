# Buyer Personas — Rondo

## Buying Journey

The typical buying journey: **Secretaris or VOG-verantwoordelijke discovers** Rondo → pitches to **Voorzitter** → Voorzitter asks **ICT-vrijwilliger** to evaluate → **Penningmeester** checks the numbers → board decides.

The website needs to serve all five personas, but the Secretaris is the front door.

---

## 1. De Secretaris (primary discoverer)

**Role:** Club secretary, handles day-to-day member administration.

**How they arrive:** Googles for solutions out of frustration — things like "sportlink export automatiseren", "ledenadministratie sportvereniging", "sportlink koppeling mailinglijst".

**Pain points:**
- Exports member data from Sportlink manually, repeatedly
- Maintains mailing lists by hand (import/export to email tool)
- No single source of truth — data in Sportlink, Excel, email, Google Drive
- Tracks VOG compliance via spreadsheets or memory
- Answers "who's on which team?" questions constantly
- Family/household relationships invisible in Sportlink

**What they need from the website:**
- Immediate recognition of their pain ("yes, this is exactly my problem")
- Clear explanation of what Rondo does and how it works
- Screenshots/demo so they can visualize the solution
- Ammunition to pitch to the board (trust signals, sponsorship model, terms)
- Easy way to try it (demo) or get in touch

**Key question:** "Does this actually solve my daily admin headache?"

---

## 2. De Voorzitter / Bestuurslid (decision maker)

**Role:** Club chair or board member, responsible for overall club governance.

**How they arrive:** Secretary sends them the link after discovering Rondo. Scans the page quickly.

**Pain points:**
- Responsible for club decisions — can't afford a bad vendor choice
- Worries about data privacy and GDPR compliance
- Concerned about vendor lock-in and continuity
- Needs to justify decisions to the general members' meeting (ALV)

**What they need from the website:**
- Credibility: who's behind this? Real person/organization?
- Trust signals: KvK registration, data processing agreement, hosting location
- Contract terms: what happens if we want to leave? Data export?
- Sustainability: will this still exist in 2 years?
- Open source reassurance (the club isn't dependent on one vendor)

**Key question:** "Is this a responsible choice for our club?"

---

## 3. De Penningmeester (budget gatekeeper)

**Role:** Club treasurer, manages finances and approves expenditures.

**How they arrive:** Forwarded the link after the board discusses Rondo. Scrolls straight to the sponsorship and costs section.

**Pain points:**
- Every euro spent on tools is a euro not spent on the sport
- Needs to fit purchases into annual budget and present to ALV
- Wary of hidden costs, price increases, or long lock-in periods
- Needs to understand the sponsorship commitment and remaining costs versus keeping manual processes

**What they need from the website:**
- Clear sponsorship terms without hidden licence fees
- What's included and which third-party costs remain with the club
- How the label, placement, duration and evaluation moment are agreed
- ROI framing: how much volunteer time does this save?

**Key question:** "What do we commit to, which external costs remain, and is it worth it?"

---

## 4. De Vrijwilligerscoordinator / VOG-verantwoordelijke (pain-driven discoverer)

**Role:** Volunteer coordinator or the person responsible for VOG (background check) compliance.

**How they arrive:** Searches specifically for VOG tracking solutions — "vog administratie sportvereniging", "vog bijhouden vrijwilligers". May also be triggered by a compliance deadline or incident.

**Pain points:**
- VOG tracking is manual, error-prone, and high-stakes
- No overview of who has a valid VOG and who doesn't
- Expiration dates slip through the cracks
- The club is at risk if non-compliant

**What they need from the website:**
- Quick validation that Rondo handles VOG tracking
- Screenshots of the VOG feature specifically
- Understanding of how it works (automatic reminders? status overview?)
- Enough to bring to the secretary/board as a recommendation

**Key question:** "Does this solve my VOG compliance nightmare?"

---

## 5. De ICT-vrijwilliger / Techneut (evaluator)

**Role:** Tech-savvy volunteer the board asks to "take a look at this tool."

**How they arrive:** Board forwards the link and asks "is this legit? Should we do this?"

**Pain points:**
- Needs to assess technical quality without deep investigation
- Concerned about integrations: does it actually connect to Sportlink, Laposta, etc.?
- Wants to know about data security, hosting, and architecture
- Wary of amateur/hobby projects that might break

**What they need from the website:**
- Technical credibility (who built it, how it's hosted)
- Integration details: what connects to what, how data flows
- Open source link (GitHub) so they can assess code quality
- Hosting and security info (EU hosting, encryption, backups)
- That the developer clearly knows what they're doing

**Key question:** "Is this technically sound and well-maintained?"
